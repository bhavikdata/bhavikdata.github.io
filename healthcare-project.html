Healthcare demand forecasting & inventory optimisation

An end-to-end analytics platform for HealthNova Global, a fictional healthcare and nutritional-products distributor. It takes raw data all the way to forecasts, reorder recommendations and an executive dashboard — and the whole pipeline runs from a clean clone in under two minutes.

All data in this project is synthetic, generated with realistic seasonality, promotions, price elasticity and stockouts. No real company or customer data is used, and the business impact figures are simulated.

The problem

Like many fast-growing distributors, the business struggled to plan stock and see what was really driving profit:

  • Stockouts on fast-moving products and excess stock on slow movers
  • Seasonal demand swings that spreadsheet planning kept missing
  • No single view of which products, regions and customers drive profit
  • Slow manual reporting and no forward-looking demand signal for purchasing

What I built

  1. Generated realistic data. Built a synthetic dataset where promotions lift demand, stockouts suppress sales and each product follows a growth, stable or declining trend.
  2. Added a data quality gate. Every run is graded PASS, WARNING or FAIL before anything downstream uses it.
  3. Designed a star schema. Fact tables for sales, inventory and returns with product, customer, warehouse, country, date and promotion dimensions — Snowflake-ready DDL included.
  4. Engineered features safely. Lag and rolling features with an explicit test that no future data leaks into training.
  5. Compared five forecasting models. Naive, moving average, linear regression, random forest and gradient boosting on a strict time-based holdout; the lowest-WAPE model is picked automatically.
  6. Turned forecasts into actions. Safety stock and reorder points per product and warehouse, each tagged Critical, High, Medium or Low risk.
  7. Segmented customers and flagged anomalies. RFM rules compared against K-Means, plus z-score, IQR and Isolation Forest anomaly detection.
  8. Presented it. An eight-page Streamlit dashboard, 25 documented SQL queries, and a Power BI guide with 20+ DAX measures.

Results

Random Forest was selected, cutting weighted error from 30.6% to 17.25% — about a 44% relative improvement over a “repeat yesterday” forecast. I used WAPE rather than MAPE because many individual days have near-zero demand, which distorts MAPE.

Key findings

  • The top 10 products bring in roughly 64% of revenue, so a small core of the 180-product catalogue drives most of the value.
  • Immune Support and Pain Relief peak in winter while Sports Nutrition peaks in summer — flat planning would understock both, in opposite seasons.
  • Revenue per transaction is lower during promotions, a reminder to measure promotion success by extra volume, not order value.
  • The overall stockout rate is about 3%, concentrated in a specific list of Critical and High-risk products rather than spread across the catalogue.

What I’d improve next

  • Reconcile category forecasts down to product level as more history builds up
  • Add Prophet or SARIMA for the strongly seasonal categories
  • Run the quality gate in an orchestrator such as Airflow, with alerts on FAIL
  • Add a proper per-country holiday calendar
