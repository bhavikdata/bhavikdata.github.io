Global supplement sales & inventory analytics

A single Excel workbook for NutriGlobal Distribution, a fictional supplement seller operating in 8 countries and 4 sales channels. A non-technical manager can open it, filter it and trust it — every number traces back to a formula.

All data in this project is synthetic and reproducible (generated with a fixed random seed). Insights and recommendations are simulated for demonstration, not real business results.

The problem

Leadership needed one workbook that answers four questions:

  • How are revenue and margin trending, and which categories, regions and channels drive them?
  • How does demand change month to month with seasonality and promotions?
  • Which products run out of stock most often, and where should replenishment focus?
  • How well can a simple, explainable model forecast next month’s demand?

What I built

  1. Generated messy raw extracts. Sales, products, inventory and geography files with realistic problems built in.
  2. Cleaned the data in Python. Removed duplicates, fixed invalid dates, standardised country names, filled missing categories and recalculated inventory balances — every fix logged on a Data Quality sheet.
  3. Modelled it in two layers. Normalised fact and dimension tables, plus a denormalised reporting layer so the dashboard stays fast.
  4. Built the dashboard. KPI cards driven by SUMIFS, Year and Category dropdown filters, 8 native charts and conditional formatting across 17 sheets.
  5. Forecast demand. A transparent seasonal-naive baseline evaluated on a 3-month holdout with MAE, RMSE and WAPE.
  6. Verified every KPI. Recomputed each headline figure independently in Python and reconciled it against the workbook.

Results

Protein is the riskiest category: it has the highest stockout rate and the thinnest margin, so it has the least cushion to absorb lost sales.

Key findings

  • Revenue grew from $4.68M in 2024 to $8.89M in 2025 (+89.8%), with the clearest peaks in April and December — the place to concentrate stock build-up and marketing.
  • Protein earned the most revenue ($5.16M) yet Whey Protein Vanilla ran out of stock 65% of the time — raise reorder points for the two whey products specifically, not across the board.
  • B2C channels brought in $7.45M vs $6.13M for B2B, and Online Retail alone $4.73M — a good leading signal for company-wide replenishment.
  • The seasonal-naive forecast scored 58.5% accuracy on the holdout. That’s an honest result: a same-month-last-year model can’t keep up with 90% growth.

What I’d improve next

  • Add a trend adjustment on top of the seasonal-naive forecast
  • Model supplier lead-time variability and a demand-based safety stock formula
  • Add native PivotTables and slicers built in desktop Excel
  • Build a Power BI or Streamlit view of the same data
